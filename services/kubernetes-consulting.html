Do You Actually Need Kubernetes?

Honest answer: not always. Here's how we think about it.

✅ Kubernetes makes sense when:

  • You have 10+ microservices to manage
  • You need consistent environments across dev/staging/prod
  • You're deploying multiple times per day
  • You need sophisticated autoscaling (HPA, VPA, KEDA)
  • You're running multi-region for resilience
  • You have dedicated platform/DevOps team capacity

❌ You might not need K8s if:

  • You have a monolith or 2-3 services — ECS/Cloud Run might be simpler
  • Your team is small (< 5 engineers) and wearing many hats
  • You deploy weekly or less
  • Your workloads are predictable (no burst traffic)
  • You're pre-product-market-fit and iterating fast

We'll tell you honestly. We've talked clients out of Kubernetes when simpler solutions fit better.

Kubernetes FAQ

EKS, GKE, or AKS — which should we choose?

GKE: Best overall Kubernetes experience, most mature, excellent autopilot mode. EKS: If you're AWS-native and want deep integration with AWS services. AKS: If you're Azure-native or need Active Directory integration. We'll recommend based on your existing cloud and team.

How long does a Kubernetes migration take?

Simple apps (< 5 services): 2-4 weeks. Medium complexity: 4-8 weeks. Large migrations (20+ services, complex data): 2-4 months. We scope precisely before starting.

What about managed services like ECS or Cloud Run?

Great alternatives! ECS is simpler if you're AWS-only. Cloud Run is excellent for stateless services. We'll honestly tell you if these are better fits than Kubernetes.

How do you handle stateful workloads?

Carefully. For databases, we usually recommend managed services (RDS, Cloud SQL) over running them in K8s. For caches and queues, we use StatefulSets with proper PVCs and backup strategies.

Do we need a service mesh?

Probably not (yet). Service meshes like Istio add complexity. We only recommend them when you need advanced traffic management, mTLS between all services, or sophisticated observability. Most teams don't need it initially.
